Step 1: Data Input Specification

Step 2: Application of Analytical Frameworks
Cynefin Framework: This framework will be used to categorize problems identified in the data into its four domains (simple, complicated, complex, chaotic). This categorization helps in understanding the nature of decision-making required.
Simple: Clear cause-and-effect relationships, where best practices are applicable.
Complicated: Cause-and-effect relationships discoverable but not immediately apparent, requiring expert analysis.
Complex: No clear cause-and-effect relationships, outcomes are unpredictable.
Chaotic: Emergency decision-making where rapid action is required without clear right answers.
BCG Growth-Share Matrix: This will help in positioning products or services in a portfolio based on their market growth rate and relative market share. The matrix has four quadrants:
Stars: High growth, high market share products.
Question Marks: High growth, low market share products.
Cash Cows: Low growth, high market share products.
Dogs: Low growth, low market share products.

Step 3: Data Analysis and Interpretation
Automated Analysis: Develop algorithms to automatically classify data points into the Cynefin framework and BCG matrix based on the data attributes provided.
Strategic Insights: The system should provide strategic recommendations based on the classification:
For simple problems, suggest standard operating procedures.
For complicated problems, recommend expert consultation or detailed analysis.
For complex scenarios, suggest innovative approaches and adaptability.
For chaotic situations, prompt for immediate, decisive actions.
For BCG categories, provide strategies like investment in Stars, possible divestment from Dogs, etc.

Step 4: Reporting and Visualization
Interactive Dashboards: Provide users with dashboards that visualize their data across the Cynefin framework and BCG matrix.
